One change, a decision of record: a write can be held to the decision it cites, before it is sent. The receipt says which policy held it, and the verifier compares what an action claims of its record. A gateway configured as before behaves as before.

## What an operator must do

- **Nothing, to keep things as they are.** A platform with no `decisionPolicy` is unchanged: `/act` reads nothing inside the record it cites, and its receipts carry no new member. Engine configurations of versions 1 to 3 are read as before.
- **To hold a write tool to its decision,** give the platform a `decisionPolicy` and declare engine configuration version `"4"`. A gateway earlier than this release refuses a version-4 file, as it refuses any version it does not know. `connect` keeps a replaced entry's policy, refuses a replacement the policy would not fit, and no longer lowers a file's version.
- **Verification can now fail where it passed.** For an action receipt whose decision record is a line of the runtime's audit trail, the verifier compares the record's pack digest and its citations with what the receipt claims, and reports `decision-pack-mismatch` or `decision-cites-mismatch` as failing findings. A store that `v0.7.0` verified can therefore fail under this release where an action claimed a pack or receipts other than its record's. Any other record is not compared, and a new `observations` member of the report says so without failing it.
- **A host that shows `/act`'s refusals has seven new steps to name.** They are `record`, `consistency`, `policy-outcome`, `policy-handoff`, `policy-packs`, `policy-reviewed` and `policy-bind`. `policy-handoff` is the one to route to a person: the decision asked for one.

## A write is held to its decision (#193, ADR-0011)

Before this release, `/act` held that the record a request names exists with the stated digest and that the receipts it cites verify. It read nothing inside the record. So a signed action receipt could name a record that had asked for a handoff, or had decided another outcome than the write assumes, or had been decided on another revision of the object written. [ADR-0011](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/v0.8.0/docs/adr/0011-hold-a-write-to-its-decision.md) decides receipt-v3's open question 5 and adds three things.

**A policy per write tool, the operator's.** A platform's `decisionPolicy` is keyed by write tool, and each policy is a closed object:

| Member | What it requires of the cited record |
| --- | --- |
| `outcomes` (required) | its disposition is an outcome in this list, and no handoff is requested |
| `packs` | its pack digest is one of these |
| `reviewed` | it says `"reviewed": true` |
| `bind` | each named argument of the write equals the named fact the decision was made on, as the same JSON value (the number `4` never equals `"4"` or `4.0`) |

For a tool with a policy, `/act` reads the record that matched the digest and refuses before anything is sent unless all of these hold:

- the record is a runtime evaluation record;
- the request's pack digest and citations are the record's;
- the record meets every member of the policy.

A refusal names its step and quotes no value from the record. The request never says what it is held to.

**The receipt names the policy.** An action receipt for a tool with a policy carries `action.policy`, the digest of that policy's canonical form, under the receipt's signature. It is absent for a tool with none. A verifier of `v0.7.0` accepts the member, since a signed member it does not know is tolerated.

**The verifier compares what an action claims of its record**, as described under "What an operator must do". `SPEC.md` §4 gains the step, and §1.2a the member. The specification now states, beside `decision`, what it does and does not establish.

What this release does not establish:

- A policy says the write matched the decision it cites. It does not say the decision was right, or that a person approved the write.
- `bind` stops a write from substituting another object or revision for the one decided on. It does not stop the object changing between the check and the commit; only a precondition on the target (a revision or an ETag compared on write) closes that interval, and that is the target's to offer.
- The records in this release's tests are written in the runtime's format by hand; the runtime's own writer is not run here.

The corpus gains five vectors: `action.policy` carried and malformed, each new finding, and a graph composite that is not compared.
