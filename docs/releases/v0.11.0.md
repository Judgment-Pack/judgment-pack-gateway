A gateway can now be a checkpoint witness (ADR-0013, delivered in the gateway): an engine configuration of version 6 may carry a `witness` member, and the signer then answers a submission of checkpoint lines and two open reads of a trail's chain, signing with the gateway's own seed under the witness's prefix. A submission token names the witness's own audience, which must differ from the engine's, and no token for the witness is taken by any other endpoint of the signer or by the MCP transport. A running witness registers and retires trails itself through two operator endpoints. A test-hygiene change to `verify-ts` rides along. A gateway that is not a witness keeps its receipts, registry, policies and connections, and nothing that started under `v0.10.1` refuses to start.

## What an operator must do

- **Nothing**, unless the gateway is to be a witness. No receipt, registry or connection format changes.
- **To be a witness**, add a `witness` member to a version-6 engine configuration: `log`, `marks`, the required `audience`, `registration` (`"operator"` by default), `submitters`, `trailsPerSubmitter` and `submissionsPerMinute`. `witness.audience` must differ from `identity.audience`, and an `identity` is required. Off Unix a witness is refused.
- **A front for other parties** passes `/witness/checkpoints` and the two reads under `/witness/trails/{trail}/` only, and keeps `POST /witness/registrations` and `POST /witness/trails/{trail}/retirement` on the operator's side: a front that passes all of `/witness/` lets an engine-audience token from another host administer the witness (SECURITY.md; SPEC.md §6).
- **TLS and read limits** are the operator's: the witness speaks plain HTTP on a loopback address, so reaching it from another host takes a TLS-terminating front, which is also where reads are bounded; the reference bounds no reader's rate.
- **Know what a credential can do** (SECURITY.md): a token of a trail's registered subject is enough to spoil that trail at that witness for good, and revoking it does not unsign the statement; an operator's credential, meaning any token the signer's other token-checked endpoints take of a subject not among `witness.submitters`, administers the witness. It is one key and one operator, not a multi-tenant service: every trail is signed under the gateway's own seed, the key of its receipts and seals. The reference names no narrower operator than "any token the engine takes" (#229).
- **To read Google grounding search results**, which redirect to the publisher, enable the one redirect policy this release knows on the web source: `adapter-web --redirect-policies=google-grounding`, or for Desk's local plan `JPACK_WEB_REDIRECT_POLICIES=google-grounding` in the companion's environment, which `gateway-connections --local-plan` carries to the `web` source. Absent, every redirect to another origin stays refused as in v0.10.1. Enabling it is not evidence about any publisher; it means the operator accepts that a grounding result may lead the reader to the publisher it names.

## Make a gateway a checkpoint witness (#213, closes #214)

An engine whose configuration carries a `witness` member opens the witness's log, marks and registrations at start and applies the start-up checks before the store and the registry are made; a witness the checks refuse leaves nothing made. It keeps every statement for as long as it runs; nothing is pruned or removed.

- **Configuration.** The member is closed and needs `identity`; with it, `platforms` may be empty, and an engine with no platform is not refused for a host runtime's socket since it runs no adapter. `log` and `marks` are absolute, clean paths; the marks are on storage apart from the log's.
- **Endpoints.** `POST /witness/checkpoints`, a bearer token of the configured issuer verified as every other endpoint verifies one, taken only for a trail registered to the token's subject; and the open reads `GET /witness/trails/{trail}/head` and `.../statements?from&limit`. A submission is held to its method, token, submitters, a rate a minute and a place among the 32 submissions in flight (4 of them one submitter's) before its body is read; the body is read through its bound (1048576 bytes, a line of 4096) and each line is held to a checkpoint's canonical form as it arrives. A witness whose writer failed answers every submission `503` until it restarts. Paths the mux would clean are refused `400 malformed` in the witness's own form.
- **Commands.** `gateway witness verify`, `register`, `retire` and `repair` act on a stopped witness's files under the locks a running witness holds; against a running witness `register`, `retire` and `repair` refuse and change nothing. `register` says which subject and issuer it changed from.
- **Submission tokens (#214).** A submission token names `witness.audience`; the witness refuses a token that names the engine's audience alone. Every other token-checked endpoint of the signer and the MCP transport refuse, with "a token for the witness is refused here", a token that names the witness's audience (with or without the engine's) or that is of a subject among `witness.submitters`.
- **Retirement and the trail bound.** A retirement frees its submitter's place in `trailsPerSubmitter`, and the start-up count skips retired trails, so a poisoned trail's operator can go on under a new one.
- Tests: 24 new test functions (`go/witness_config_test.go`, `go/witness_serve_test.go`, `go/witness_cmd_test.go`) go through the signer's own handler with a recorder, every status and reason and every bound at and one past; two serve over an in-memory connection (an answer nobody reads is abandoned at its bound; statements are fetched a page at a time and read back by the reader with no finding); one needs a real listener and skips where none can be opened. CI's verify-ts job reads a served chain through the process contract. No corpus vector holds the endpoints' requests and answers; `gateway conform` is unchanged.
- What it establishes: the witness tells its operator from a submitter by the token's audience and subject, a submitter's token opens the witness and nothing else, and a statement is what SPEC.md §6 says a statement is. A read reveals, for a trail with statements, every statement to anyone who names the trail; a trail with none, registered or never named, is the same `404`, and no read serves a registration, a submitter or a list of trails.
- What it does not: it bounds no reader's rate; it is not multi-tenant; it does not stop a credential of a registered subject from spoiling its trail at this witness for good; a witness whose log or marks are damaged refuses to start, and the rules it recovers by can cost it its key, which is the gateway's key for receipts and seals too. The `409 index-bound` refusal (a trail at index 2^53-2) is not reached by any test.

## Bound the witness vector's failure message and build large inputs as buffers (#227, closes #217)

Test-only: no change to `verify-ts/src/`, CI or `go/`. The vector test's failure message is built only on failure and bounded to the vector's name plus the first 2,000 characters of the answer; `corpus.test.ts` builds its two 33-million-escape documents as Buffers and runs under a 12 GB address-space cap (10 pass, 0 fail, 0 skipped).

## Register and retire a trail on a running witness (#228, closes #215)

- `POST /witness/registrations` (`application/json` `{"trail","issuer","subject"}`) answers `200` with `changedFrom` naming the registration it replaced, or `null`. `POST /witness/trails/{trail}/retirement` (no body) answers `200` with the retirement statement. `register` and `retire` still act on a stopped witness only, and their refusal against a running one now names the endpoint; `repair` stays offline.
- Both verify the token under the engine's audience, as `/seal` does; a submitter's token (the witness's audience, alone or with the engine's, or a listed submitter's subject) and an absent token are `401` with `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer`. The registration is held to the offline command's rule, its strings are read by the gateway's own parser (a subject that is not UTF-8 or holds a lone surrogate is `400`), and one the same as the held registration is answered `200` and appends nothing.
- A registration is synced, and a retirement durable and marked, before the `200`; the retirement is the offline command's own statement bytes and order. A trail with no statement is `404 unknown-trail`, one already retired `409 retired`, a stopped writer `503 stopped`. Each act takes a place among the 32 in flight and is not counted against `submissionsPerMinute`.
- Tests (`go/witness_admin_test.go`): registration then acceptance; a live retirement compared byte for byte with `gateway witness retire` on a copy of the same files, logs and marks identical afterwards; the operator checks on both endpoints; the refusals; the seam's order of writes; a 19-case crash matrix in which each case answers `503`, never `200`.
- What it establishes: in SPEC.md §6's words, the witness tells its operator from a submitter by the token's audience and subject alone, so any token the signer's other token-checked endpoints take is the operator's here, as it is at `/seal`; a submitter's credential cannot register or retire anything; a registration is never in a statement, and no read or submission serves it. What it does not: any narrower operator than "any token the engine takes" (#229, open: an operators list); repair on a running witness.

## Follow a Google grounding redirect to its publisher, when the operator permits it (#231, closes #230)

- A grounding search result is a URL on `https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/<token>` that redirects to the publisher. v0.10.1 refused that hop as it refuses every redirect to another origin. Under the operator's `google-grounding` policy the web source follows one and only one cross-origin hop: the request's origin is exactly the grounding endpoint on port 443, with a path beginning `/grounding-api-redirect/`, a non-empty token and no query; the hop is the first redirect; its destination passes the same admission as any selected origin (`https`, port 443, a public address after DNS) and is not the provider. Every later hop is held to the publisher's origin as v0.10.1 holds it. A redirect to another origin whose destination does not resolve, or resolves to any private address, is refused as unavailable or private before anything else is decided, with the policy on or off, so no refusal ever names a private destination. No cookie, authorization or referrer header is sent on any hop, and a `site`-scoped discovery read never follows the policy hop.
- The fixed token `web-redirect-origin` stays. The source's error line may now carry one member, `{"redirect":{"from":<origin>,"to":<origin>,"policy":"google-grounding"|null,"permitted":false}}`, origins only, present when the line fits the 200 bytes the gateway relays and never on an unconditional refusal (a private address, an invalid URL): `policy` names the policy that would cover the hop, so a consumer can tell a policy block from a hard block and offer a configuration review only where one exists. The record keeps `requestedURL` and the final `URL` and carries `provenance.source.redirectPolicy` when the hop was followed, within attachment version 1. `gateway-connections --source-capabilities` says which policies the web source supports and which are enabled. The consumer contract is in `docs/design/public-web-sources.md`.
- Tests: with the policy absent, a synthetic grounding endpoint answering `302` to a publisher is refused with exactly one request made and the publisher never contacted; with it, the read completes and the record names both URLs and the policy; an ordinary cross-origin redirect, a wrong path, an empty token, a query, a lookalike host, a second cross-origin hop, a return to the provider, `http`, another port, URL credentials, a private literal, a private DNS answer and a discovery read are each refused with their existing tokens; a request cannot enable a policy; an unknown policy name refuses the start.
- What it establishes: the bytes of a snapshot came from the origin the operator selected, or, under the policy, from the publisher a grounding result named, and the record says which. What it does not: anything about that publisher's content; configuration is not evidence.
